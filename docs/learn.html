Why the first word is slow and the rest are fast

A language model works in two phases.

1. Prefill (reading). The model reads your whole question at once before it can say anything. The time this takes is TTFT, time to first token. A bigger model or a longer question means a longer wait.

2. Decode (writing). The model then writes one token at a time, each taking about the same time. That time is TPOT, time per output token.

A token is a piece of a word, about 4 letters of English. "Inference" might be two tokens.

Total time ≈ TTFT + (tokens × TPOT)
305 + (40 × 27) ≈ 1,385 ms

Your benchmark measured 1,415 ms, so the formula holds. Being able to do this sum in an interview shows you know where latency comes from.

Reading your benchmark numbers

The benchmark fires many requests at the same time, like many users, and times each one. It then sorts the times from fastest to slowest.

p50 is the middle one: a typical user. p95 is near the slow end: 1 in 20 users waits at least this long. Companies watch p95 because those are the users who complain.

Throughput is the total words per second across all users together. On a real GPU you pay by the hour, so higher throughput means cheaper words.
